How to Build an SEO-Friendly Website with MYC.DEV

The first prompt you give MYC.DEV can shape how easily customers find what they need. If you’re building a coffee shop website, someone looking for the menu should land on a page with drinks and prices. Someone looking for opening hours should find the address and hours on a dedicated Visit page.

In this walkthrough, we’ll build a new site for Maple & Mug Coffee, a fictional shop in St. Charles, Missouri. You can adapt the same steps to a service business, portfolio, or online store.

1. Plan your pages around customer questions

Customer question Page What to put on it
What kind of coffee shop is this? Home, / A short introduction, the city, and links to Menu and Visit
What can I order and what does it cost? Menu, /menu Drinks, prices, and seasonal specials as readable text
When are you open and how do I get there? Visit, /visit Hours, street address, directions, and contact details

Use the pages your customers actually need. For a service business, your main service could have its own page with the service area, what’s included, and how to book.

2. Include SEO requirements in your first MYC.DEV prompt

Start a new website project in MYC.DEV. Paste the prompt below and replace the example business and pages with your own details.

Build a mobile-friendly website for Maple & Mug Coffee, an independent coffee shop in St. Charles, Missouri. Create a homepage at /, a menu page at /menu, and a visit page at /visit.

On the homepage, describe the shop and link to Menu and Visit. On the menu page, display the menu items and prices I provide as readable text, grouped by drink type. On the visit page, show the address, hours, and contact details I provide. Flag any missing business details for me to fill in before publishing.

Give each public page a distinct page title, a concise meta description, and a descriptive main heading. Use descriptive navigation links and meaningful alt text for images that convey information.

Set up an XML sitemap for the public pages and a robots.txt file that allows those pages to be crawled. Use my preferred live domain for the sitemap and canonical URLs when I provide it. Make the main page content available without a login or an interaction.

A page title identifies the page in the browser and can influence the title Google shows in search. A meta description summarizes the page and may be used as the search snippet. Give each page its own title and description so they reflect what the visitor will actually find. Google’s SEO Starter Guide

3. Review the generated site before publishing

Use the MYC.DEV preview to open each page, check the navigation, and review the layout on mobile. Replace placeholder business details with verified information.

Menu page element Example
Page title Coffee Menu in St. Charles · Maple & Mug
Main heading Maple & Mug Coffee Menu
Meta description Explore espresso drinks and seasonal specials at Maple & Mug Coffee in St. Charles. See the menu and prices.
Link from Home View the coffee menu

If the menu appears as an image, refine the generated project with a follow-up prompt.

In this project, display the menu items and prices as readable text on /menu using the details I provide. Keep the existing visual style, and add a “View the coffee menu” link from the homepage.

4. Publish and verify your website

Publish your website and open it in a browser. Check that the public pages work on your live domain and that /sitemap.xml lists the right live URLs. Open /robots.txt and check that your intended public pages can be crawled.

Then verify your website in Google Search Console.

  1. Open Sitemaps and submit the live sitemap URL.
  2. Paste a priority page’s full URL into URL Inspection.
  3. Select Test Live URL to check whether Google can access the page.
  4. Request indexing for a newly published priority page. Google decides when and whether to index it.

Once data is available, use Search Console’s Performance report to see which searches generate impressions and clicks. If visitors find your site by searching for opening hours, make those hours easy to spot on your Visit page. Google’s sitemap guide · Google’s indexing guide · Google’s Search Console guide
